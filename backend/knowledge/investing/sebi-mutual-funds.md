# Mutual Funds and SEBI Guidelines

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) regulates the securities market, including mutual funds, to protect investor interests.

## Types of Mutual Funds
SEBI has categorized mutual funds to ensure uniformity:

### Equity Funds
Invest primarily in stocks. They carry higher risk but offer the potential for higher long-term returns.
- **Large Cap:** Invest in the top 100 companies by market capitalization. Lower risk than mid/small caps.
- **Mid Cap:** Invest in 101st to 250th companies. Medium risk.
- **Small Cap:** Invest in companies beyond 250th. Very high risk.
- **ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme):** The only mutual fund that qualifies for tax deduction under Section 80C. It has a mandatory lock-in period of 3 years.

### Debt Funds
Invest in fixed-income securities like government bonds, corporate bonds, and treasury bills. They are less volatile than equity funds and are suited for conservative investors seeking capital preservation and regular income.

### Hybrid Funds
Invest in a mix of equity and debt to balance risk and return.

## Investor Rights
Under SEBI guidelines:
- Investors have the right to receive dividend/redemption proceeds within a specified timeframe (typically 3-10 working days).
- Funds must disclose their NAV (Net Asset Value) daily.
- Scheme Information Documents (SID) must clearly outline the fund's objective, risks, and expense ratios.
