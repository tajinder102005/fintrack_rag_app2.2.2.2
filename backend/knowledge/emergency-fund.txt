# Emergency Fund Guide

An emergency fund is money set aside to cover unexpected expenses or financial emergencies. This could include medical bills, urgent car repairs, home appliances breaking down, or a sudden loss of income.

## Why Do You Need an Emergency Fund?
Life is unpredictable. Without an emergency fund, unexpected expenses can force you to rely on high-interest credit cards or loans, leading to a cycle of debt. An emergency fund acts as a financial shock absorber.

## How Much Should You Maintain?
- **General Rule**: It should generally cover 3 to 6 months of your essential living expenses.
- **Income Stability**: The appropriate amount depends on income stability. If you have a very stable job (e.g., government employee), 3 months might be sufficient. 
- **Irregular Income**: Individuals with irregular income (freelancers, business owners) may consider saving 6 to 12 months of expenses to handle leaner periods.

## Where to Keep It?
Your emergency fund should be easily accessible but separate from your daily checking account. High-yield savings accounts or liquid mutual funds are often good choices because they offer some return while keeping the money accessible without penalties.
